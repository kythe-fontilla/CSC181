## 1. User group

**Who are you designing for?**  
(1) small local businesses, especially pop-up stalls and new vendors, and 
(2) customers in Iligan, namely residents, students, and visitors who want to try local foods and services during Diyandi Festival.


**Why might this group need support during Diyandi?**  
Diyandi brings a surge of temporary pop-up stalls and seasonal businesses. Vendors need a way to get noticed by a large crowd, and customers want a wide range of options they can browse at their own convenience. At the moment, there is no single place where both sides can meet. A platform would help customers easily locate stalls and even order through delivery, while giving vendors visibility they would not otherwise have.


---

## 2. Situation or need

**What is this group trying to do during Diyandi?**  
- **Business owners:** promote their products and reach more customers.
- **Customers:** discover, locate, and order from food stalls and service providers during the festival.

---

## 3. Problem or inconvenience

**What may make this task difficult, confusing, unsafe, slow, or inconvenient?**  
Pop-ups are common during the festival, but new and small business owners struggle to promote their products because customers have little to no knowledge of them. From my own experience of three years living in Iligan, I know many businesses open during Diyandi. However, I have no platform to see where these stalls are or what they offer, so I usually settle for familiar, non-seasonal options like Jollibee and similar chains. As a result, customers miss out on local, festival-only food, and small vendors lose potential sales.

**Assumption:** many other residents, students, and visitors face the same problem, especially visitors who are unfamiliar with the city.

---

## 4. Proposed digital solution

**What digital tool would you propose?**  
A mobile application and a website, as a festival marketplace and directory.

- **For customers:** browse stalls and products, see their locations on a map, and order through delivery.
- **For vendors:** create a simple free profile, post their menu or products, and share their stall location and operating hours.

A mobile app is best for people on the go at the festival, while a website makes the platform accessible to anyone, including visitors who don't want to install an app. Together they bridge the gap between new small vendors and customers who want to try something local.

**How would it help the intended users?**  
- **For small vendors and pop-up owners:** They get a free platform to advertise their products, post their menu, and show their stall location, even if they are new to the market and have no existing following.

- **For customers (residents, students, and visitors):** Instead of settling for familiar, non-seasonal options like Jollibee, they can see which stalls are open during the festival, browse what each one offers, and locate them on a map. If they can't go in person, they can order through delivery at their own convenience. 

---

## 5. Things users can do

Describe **two specific actions** that users could perform using your proposed system.

1. A vendor or pop-up owner can create a post to advertise their business. The post can include their products or menu, prices, photos, stall location, and operating hours, so customers can find them even if the business is new to the market.
2. A customer can browse the list of stalls and products available during Diyandi, view where each one is located, and place an order through delivery, all from their phone and at their own convenience.

## 6. Important qualities

Identify **two qualities** that would make your proposed system useful. You may consider whether it should be easy to use, fast, reliable, safe, private, accessible, multilingual, low-data, clear, or available during high demand.

### Quality 1: Free

**Why does this matter to users?**  
The application gives small business owners a free platform to promote their products. A free platform lets even the smallest stall get noticed during Diyandi and compete fairly with more established businesses. It also means customers see a wider range of local options, since more vendors are able to participate.


### Quality 2: Accessible

**Why does this matter to users?**  
Customers can browse a wide variety of choices using just their gadgets, wherever they are. The delivery option also makes stores easy to reach, even for people who are busy, far from the venue, or unable to go out during the crowded festival days. This makes it easier for residents, students, and visitors to try local, festival-only food and services at their own convenience.
